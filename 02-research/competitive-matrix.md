# Competitive Matrix: Streak, Habit & Micro-Learning Apps

*Context: Streakly's Day-7 retention dropped from 48% to 39%; most week-1 streak-breakers never return. This benchmarks how comparable apps handle streaks and post-break re-engagement.*

## Duolingo

- **Core features:** Gamified language lessons, leaderboards/leagues, streak counter, Streak Society (milestone club at 7/30/100/365 days), AI-powered personalized practice, offline access (paid tiers).
- **Pricing:** Free; Lite $35.99/yr; Super $16.99/mo or $119.99/yr (individual); Family ~$119.99–143.99/yr; Max (AI features) ~$168/yr.
- **Target customer:** Mass-market, casual language learners across a broad age range who respond to gamification.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Streak + leagues + Streak Society milestones; streak freezes purchasable with in-app currency (Gems); push notifications widely described as aggressive/"nagging." In June 2026, ran a month-long global campaign letting users with a lost streak of 30+ days restore their longest streak by completing 3 lessons — but this was a limited-time, one-off revival event, not a standing per-user comeback flow.
- **Notable recent changes:** June 2026 streak-restoration campaign; expanded CEFR content in nine languages; new free "Speaking Adventures" scenario practice; a practice hub for repeating past lessons.

## Babbel

- **Core features:** Structured courses built by human teachers (not AI-generated), spaced-repetition Review Manager, speech-recognition practice, offline downloads, Babbel Live (real-teacher group/private classes).
- **Pricing:** ~$8.95/mo on annual plan (single language, $107.40/yr); month-to-month ~$14.99/mo; one-time Lifetime plan $299 (all languages, frequently discounted to $170–200); 7-day free trial.
- **Target customer:** More serious adult learners who want structured, teacher-designed courses over casual gamified play, typically committed to one language.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Has a streak counter, but it resets to zero on a missed day with no freeze or recovery mechanic found. Deliberately low-pressure by design — no streak-loss notification, no leaderboard, no guilt-trip character. Uses scheduled learning reminders (push, device-linked) timed to the user's habits rather than frequent nudges.
- **Notable recent changes:** No major 2026 product change surfaced in research beyond continued Babbel Live expansion and standard pricing promotions; the low-pressure, no-guilt notification philosophy is its standing differentiator versus Duolingo.

## Elevate

- **Core features:** 40+ brain-training games covering math, memory, reading, vocabulary, and communication skills; personalized adaptive training program; 150+ achievements; workout calendar visualizing consistency.
- **Pricing:** Free tier (limited to 3 games/day, no skill choice); ~$9.99/mo or $39.99/yr for full access.
- **Target customer:** Adults focused on cognitive fitness and practical communication skills, not tied to a specific subject or language.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Daily streak + achievements + workout calendar; personalized program adapts over time. No streak-freeze or post-break recovery mechanic surfaced in research.
- **Notable recent changes:** Continued emphasis on personalized, adaptive training programs ("personalized brain training to start 2026 strong"); no specific new comeback or recovery feature found.

## Mimo

- **Core features:** Real in-app coding environment (write and run actual code, not just multiple choice); tracks in Python, JavaScript, HTML/CSS, SQL, TypeScript; XP, achievement badges, and a visual progress path.
- **Pricing:** Free tier (limited); Mimo Max ~$24.99/mo billed annually; 7-day free trial.
- **Target customer:** Coding beginners, students supplementing coursework, professionals upskilling or switching into tech, and self-described "habit-builders" who like 5-minute daily bursts, streaks, and XP.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Streak + "streak shields" that protect a user's streak on a missed day — a built-in forgiveness mechanic baked into the core loop, not a one-off campaign.
- **Notable recent changes:** 2025 curriculum refresh added SQL and TypeScript tracks alongside the core Python/JS/HTML/CSS courses.

## White Space for Streakly

**1. A standing, personalized comeback flow — not just breakage prevention.** Mimo and Duolingo both help users avoid losing a streak in the first place (shields, paid freezes). Duolingo's only response to an *already-broken* streak was a one-off, limited-time marketing campaign, not a permanent feature. Babbel and Elevate offer no recovery mechanic at all. None of the four have an always-on, per-user flow that meets someone right after they've broken a streak with something specific to their own progress — which is exactly the gap Streakly's own hypothesis (a "Comeback screen" with a best-streak stat, a short comeback lesson, and a one-tap freeze) is aimed at.

**2. Notification tone/timing that adapts to where the user is in their journey.** Duolingo leans aggressive and gamified; Babbel deliberately dialed back to low-pressure — but both apply one philosophy to everyone, regardless of tenure. Research didn't surface any competitor that varies notification tone or content based on whether a user is a brand-new, still-fragile learner versus an established one. This lines up with Streakly's own NPS finding that notifications feel "random" and generic — an opportunity to calibrate messaging by user stage rather than a single blanket policy.
